# DR-05 — Retail API/algo compliance and OPS interpretation

**Researcher:** Codex, `/root/finish_rules_compliance`. **Date/access date:** 2026-11-05, Asia/Kolkata. **Scope:** public SEBI/NSE requirements relevant to a possible own-account NIFTY API route; current project remains offline. **Status:** RESEARCH RETURN — PENDING INDEPENDENT COMPLIANCE/SOURCE REVIEW. This report does not determine that the user, broker account or repository is compliant.

## Executive finding

Primary SEBI text confirms the retail-algo framework's mandatory applicability to stock brokers from **2026-04-01**. Primary NSE implementation standards establish a **10 OPS per exchange** threshold measured in the **broker server's calendar-clock second**, with broker enforcement; they do not, in the reviewed text, fully define every message-counting edge case. API keys and IPs must not be treated as separate entitlements to evade the threshold.

The public NSE page updated 2026-11-03 now directs prospective algo providers to **NSE/INVG/73992 dated 2026-04-30**. That document and full INVG69255/70309 were inaccessible here. Therefore the 2025 standards/FAQ cannot be certified as the complete current rules. Their directly verified content is retained with scope and amendment gaps. Full current interpretation, account policy, credentials, entitlement, data retention and route acceptance remain owner/reviewer blockers. No value is promoted to a production registry.

## 3. Applicability and registration paths

SEBI0000013 §5 I(c) distinguishes self-developed tech-savvy retail algorithms: individual strategy registration through the broker is triggered above the specified threshold. This is not a general exemption from tagging, broker RMS, API access controls or other rules. INVG67858 B3 describes the generic exchange algo-ID treatment below threshold; C1–2 describes registration through the broker above threshold. A generic ID is not a strategy-specific approval.

For broker-provided algos, INVG67858 D1–2 provides exchange registration/ID and client order tagging. For outside algo providers, SEBI0000013 §5 I/III places broker/vendor in principal/agent roles with exchange empanelment and broker due diligence; black-box provider requirements include RA registration and documented research (§5 V). The reviewed standards do not authorize distributing the owner's private strategy to others. Owner-only and family use distinctions require account/use-case confirmation before applicability is recorded.

INVG69289 identifies genuinely manual member IBT/STWT front ends separately from Client Direct API. Automatically generated API orders cannot be relabeled manual merely because the owner clicked “run” or because order flow is low. The precise broker product/category is an owner input. No portal was inspected.

## 5. Order types, price protection and cancels

FAOP69296 §1 and effective clause establish NFO pre-emptive rejection of algo market orders from 2025-08-04, while §2 validates NNF category against Algo-ID. The reviewed FAQ Q11 separately cites a commodity IOC restriction; **it does not establish a blanket NFO IOC ban**. Conversely, absence of a ban is not account-specific permission.

The current futures pre-open page expressly permits algo market orders in its initial five-minute phase (DR01), while the older general NFO rule/FAQ prohibits algo market orders. Treat this as a **phase/date-scoping conflict requiring detailed later circular review**, not permission for market orders in continuous trading. The fact that a broker may convert a market request under Market Price Protection is not proof its API request category complies with the exchange rule. Current Angel MPP conversion, protection bands, stop variants, IOC, order rejections and cancel/replace policies need current official/account evidence from DR02/08.

Risk-reducing orders and cancels need protected access and reserved budget capacity in a future design, but cannot be claimed exempt from broker or exchange restrictions. “Cancel requested,” “cancel acknowledged,” and “cancel complete” are different events. A limiter must not release working-order exposure when it queues a cancellation. See DR13 for the offline gateway contract; no cancel or order was sent.
